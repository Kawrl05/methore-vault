# Karl — Research Questions: How the Topic Evolved

The source material shows this topic being reworked across three separate documents. This note tracks that progression so the reasoning isn't lost.

## Draft 1 — Midterm Topic Proposal (single RQ)
> To what extent does training through a gamified phishing-identification simulation improve graduating college students' ability to accurately identify malicious emails, links, and attachments compared to traditional awareness methods such as seminars or infographics?

This is the version carried into [[Karl Overview]] as the primary research question, paired with three objectives (accuracy comparison, red-flag category breakdown, training-time comparison).

## Draft 2 — "Research Questions Activity" (expanded to three RQs)
The same core topic was later expanded into **three parallel research questions**, each with its own objective set:

1. **Effectiveness (same as Draft 1):** gamified simulation vs. traditional methods on detection accuracy.
   - Compare pre/post accuracy between groups.
   - Identify which red-flag categories show the largest gains per method.
   - Measure time-to-competency per method.
2. **Retention over time:** how does phishing-detection *skill retention* compare between the two methods?
   - Measure decay rate of detection accuracy at multiple follow-up intervals.
   - Test whether spaced/repeated gameplay produces a slower decay curve than a single seminar.
   - Identify which specific skills (URL inspection, sender verification, attachment analysis) are retained longest vs. forgotten fastest.
3. **Individual differences as moderators:** do prior digital literacy, phishing exposure, or cybersecurity knowledge change which method works better for a given student?
   - Compare accuracy gains for low- vs. high-baseline digital literacy students.
   - Test whether prior real-world phishing exposure predicts better performance in one method over the other.
   - Identify individual traits correlated with faster mastery in the game format vs. the traditional format.

## What this means
The effectiveness question (RQ1) is the one that made it into both the formal topic proposal and the [[Shikibetsu SRS Overview|Shikibetsu SRS]]. RQ2 (retention) and RQ3 (individual differences) represent natural **follow-up studies** — they're not abandoned, just out of scope for the current one-term project (see the Time constraint in [[Shikibetsu SRS Overview]]).
