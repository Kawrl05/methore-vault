# Karl — Gamified Phishing-Identification Simulation & Detection Accuracy

## Research Question
To what extent does training through a gamified phishing-identification simulation improve graduating college students' ability to accurately identify malicious emails, links, and attachments compared to traditional awareness methods such as seminars or infographics?

> This question evolved across drafts — see [[Karl Research Questions Evolution]] for how it changed and what alternate research questions were considered.

## Background
Cybersecurity awareness programs assume that more knowledge means safer behavior — but recent data on graduating students contradicts that. Reported employment scams doubled in 2025, and Gen Z applicants fell victim at more than double the rate of Gen X (32% vs. 15%) ([[Zaza 2026]]). [[Rosano et al 2026]] measured high average cybersecurity awareness (5.43/7) among 403 Filipino college students, yet found **no significant relationship** between that awareness and safe link-handling behavior (p = 0.098). This is the [[Awareness-Behavior Gap]].

Some evidence points toward repeated, applied exposure as a corrective rather than more awareness content:
- [[Gordon et al 2019]] tracked six US healthcare institutions running phishing simulations over multiple years and recorded declining click rates as exposure accumulated.
- [[Garba et al 2020]] reported phishing click rates dropping from over 50% to 13% across a university training cycle in Nigeria (instructional sequence details need verification against the original source).

Both point toward [[Phishing Simulation Training Effectiveness|repeated, applied exposure]] as a driver of improvement, but neither isolates *what specifically* produces the effect — which is the gap this topic targets.

## Objectives
1. Measure and compare pre-test/post-test phishing-detection accuracy between a gamified-simulation group and a traditional-seminar/infographic group.
2. Determine which red-flag categories (spoofed domains, sender impersonation, suspicious attachments, urgency-based language) show the largest gains under each method.
3. Measure the training time each method needs to reach a matched competency threshold.
