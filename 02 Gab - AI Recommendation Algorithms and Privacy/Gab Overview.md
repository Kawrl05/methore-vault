# Gab — AI-Driven Recommendation Algorithms: Data & Privacy Risks

## Research Question
What risks do AI-driven algorithms, used in various social media platforms, pose to users, and what tradeoffs in data privacy and security are being given in exchange for personalization?

## Background
Social media platforms curate content to maximize time-on-site. [[Narayanan 2023]] traces this shift from manually weighted ranking (e.g., Facebook in 2010) to machine-learning systems that automatically learn what keeps users engaged (Facebook by 2018). These systems can infer sensitive attributes — like the Netflix example where users suspected race-based thumbnail personalization despite Netflix stating it never collects race/ethnicity data — without users ever explicitly providing that data.

This tension is what [[Ojokoh et al 2025]] (citing [[Klostermann Kluy 2025]]) calls the **[[Privacy-Personalization Tradeoff]]**: the same data that keeps engagement high also opens the door to privacy violations and profiling.

## Objectives
- Identify risks AI-driven algorithms pose to users through content recommendation.
- Identify what privacy/data tradeoffs users knowingly and unknowingly accept to keep using a platform.
- Evaluate how transparent platforms are about their AI-driven data use.
