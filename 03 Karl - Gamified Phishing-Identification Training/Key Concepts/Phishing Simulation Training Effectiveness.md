# Phishing Simulation Training Effectiveness

## Definition
Evidence that repeated, applied exposure to simulated phishing scenarios — rather than one-time awareness content — improves real detection behavior.

## Key Details
- [[Gordon et al 2019]]: six US healthcare institutions running phishing simulations over multiple years saw declining click rates as exposure accumulated (combined with organizational reminders, so exposure and reinforcement aren't isolated from each other).
- [[Garba et al 2020]]: a university training cycle in Nigeria saw click rates drop from over 50% to 13% (the precise instructional sequence behind this needs verification against the original source).
- Both studies support "repeated exposure works," but **neither isolates gamification as the active ingredient** — this is exactly the [[Karl Research Gap|research gap]] Karl's topic targets.
